# Using Nicene Scholar day to day

1. Open the project: click Nicene Scholar in your ChatGPT sidebar, then start a new chat inside it.
2. Start each study fresh: use a new chat per passage or topic so answers stay focused. Every chat in the project follows the instructions.
3. On your phone: open the project from the sidebar in the ChatGPT app. Same account, same project.
4. Ask well: name the passage and what you want, e.g. "Walk me through Romans 8:28-30 - Greek words, background, and what scholars say." Follow up with "go deeper on that Greek word" or "what would a first-century reader have noticed?"
5. Change translation anytime: "Use the ESV for this chat" (NIV is the default).
6. Fringe views only when you want them: say "include fringe views" and they'll come in a separate, labeled section.
7. Check the sources: answers end with a Sources list. Click any web links it gives, and treat anything it says it couldn't verify as unconfirmed.
8. Bring your own material: attach a sermon outline, study notes, or a PDF to a chat, or add it to the project's files, and ask questions about it.
9. Tweak it later: open the project's ••• menu > Project settings and edit the instructions.
10. Share it (optional): you can share the project with up to 10 people, choosing "Only those invited" or "Anyone with a link". Sharing switches it to project-only memory.

## Example questions

- Explain the historical background of the Gospel of John.
- What do mainstream scholars say about who wrote the Pastoral Epistles?
- How does the Nicene Creed's teaching on the Trinity come from Scripture?
- Show me fringe views on the Exodus, labeled as fringe.
