# Quality standard: lessons, handouts, and series

This standard defines good teaching material from Moses: lessons, handouts, and series plans, built only when asked. Everything in [biblical-research.md](biblical-research.md) applies, plus the teaching items below.

## Scope

- **In scope:** lesson plans, handouts, series maps, and teacher coaching notes.
- **Out of scope:** research answers on their own, which are covered in [biblical-research.md](biblical-research.md).

## Inputs

- An explicit request for teaching material, such as "make this a lesson," "make a handout," or "plan a 6-week series."
- The passage or theme, the audience or age group, and the class length.

## Output

- **Lesson plan:** title, passage, one-sentence goal, opening hook, background, walk-through of the text, 4–6 discussion questions, application, closing prayer prompt, timing guide, and Sources.
- **Handout:** clean printable text, such as a one-page outline, a timeline, a map described in words, or a fill-in-the-blank sheet.
- **Series:** a week-by-week map, with one line per week giving the passage and the big idea.
- **Teacher notes** are kept separate from student handouts.

## Definition of done (checklist)

- [ ] **Every research-standard item is met:** Nicene consistency, real citations, NIV labeled, mainstream scholarship by default, fringe views only on request and labeled, Greek and Hebrew backed by a lexicon, and sourced background.
- [ ] **Built on request only.** Teaching material is never offered in place of a research answer.
- [ ] **A clear learning goal** in one sentence, fitting the stated age group or audience.
- [ ] **An accurate passage summary** that matches the text and its context.
- [ ] **Discussion and application questions** suited to the audience, with 4–6 for a lesson.
- [ ] **Age-appropriate language** for the children, youth, adults, or new believers named.
- [ ] **A timing plan** that adds up to the class length given.
- [ ] **No copyrighted text beyond short, attributed quotes.** Watch the NIV quotation limits in Biblica's permission terms, and prefer references and short quotes over long printed passages. Don't reproduce long excerpts from commentaries or curricula.
- [ ] **Handouts stand alone when printed.** Each page has a title, the passage reference, the translation label, and sources, with no "see above" or chat-only references.
- [ ] **Teacher notes are kept separate** from student handouts.
- [ ] **Ends with Sources.**

## Quality bar

A teacher should be able to print it and teach it without further work, and every fact in it should survive a check.

## Common failure modes

| Miss | Why it hurts | Prevention |
|------|--------------|------------|
| Any research-standard failure, such as an invented source or missing citation | The error reaches a whole class | Run the research checklist first |
| Vague or missing goal | The lesson drifts | Write a one-sentence goal fitted to the audience |
| Wrong reading level | Kids get lost, or adults feel talked down to | Match vocabulary to the audience named |
| Timing doesn't add up | Class runs over or short | Make the timing guide add up to the class length |
| Long NIV or commentary passages printed | Copyright risk | Use short, attributed quotes, and references instead |
| Handout depends on chat context | It's useless once printed | Make each page self-contained, with the reference, translation, and sources |
| Teacher notes mixed into the student sheet | It spoils answers and confuses students | Use separate sections or files |

## Review notes

- An invented source is a fail. A missing citation is at least a "fix before sharing," as in the research standard.
- Copyright overreach, such as long NIV passages or other copyrighted text, is at least a "fix before sharing."
- Print check: read the handout on its own, without the chat, and confirm it makes sense.
- Have someone review lessons and handouts before sharing them beyond your own study.
